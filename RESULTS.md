# Results: dishonest workers at scale (M8–M10)

Everything here was measured on 4 CPU cores (no GPU) and is reproducible with the commands
shown. Tables are generated by the benchmark scripts from their saved results, not retyped.
Numbers that need a GPU or a model download are listed under [Not measured yet](#not-measured-yet).

## Setup

`python -m bench.bench_scale` trains each workload with 10 workers simulated against the real
coordinator (`seedling/runtime/simulate.py`). Three of the ten (30%) attack, or none do. The
coordinator uses one of three defenses:

| defense | what it does | work per committed pair |
|---|---|---|
| none | centered ranks only | 1x |
| median3 | every pair scored by 3 workers, coordinate-wise median, no bans: the robust-aggregation approach of CyBeR-0 (Egger et al. 2025) | 3x |
| spot | 10% spot checks, probation 10, vote and ban (M8) | about 1.1x |

| attack | what the dishonest workers do |
|---|---|
| flip | score honestly, then swap F+ and F- (pushes the model the wrong way) |
| lazy | skip the work and send zeros (free riders) |
| sleeper | honest through probation (20 pairs), then flip |

Lies are deterministic, so dishonest workers collude for free: two flippers that score the
same pair report the same lie.

| workload | model | steps x pairs | metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST | MLP, 0.1M parameters | 300 x 50 | test accuracy |
| HalfCheetah-v5 (MuJoCo) | tanh MLP 17-32-6 | 150 x 32 | return over 1000 steps, 5 held-out starts |
| Character GPT | TinyGPT, 4 layers x 128, 0.8M trained parameters, trained from scratch by low-rank ES alone | 200 x 32 | validation loss (nats per character) |

The HalfCheetah dynamics and reward match gymnasium's `HalfCheetah-v5` bit for bit (tested).
The character GPT is the recipe of EGGROLL (Sarkar et al. 2025): rank-1 noise on every Linear
layer, here with exact int32 updates.

## What the numbers say

- **Without a defense, flippers and sleepers hurt every workload, and gracefully.** Scores
  become coefficients through centered ranks, so a lie counts only through its rank: m
  dishonest pairs out of N move the update by a bounded amount whatever they report
  (`influence_bound`, M8). Flipping is the most harmful bounded lie. Lazy workers' zeros
  tie at the top of the ranking and nearly cancel, so they mostly cost the information in
  their pairs: 2.6 points on MNIST, within seed noise on the GPT.
- **Spot checks cost 1.1x and stop flip and lazy completely.** 48 of the 48 liars in those
  rows were banned, 0 honest workers were, and 16 of the 16 runs ended
  bit-identical to the all-honest run with the same seed: no lie reached the journal.
- **Replication with a median costs 3x and still lets about 18% of pairs through as lies.**
  That is exactly the chance that at least 2 of a pair's 3 scorers are colluders when 3 of
  10 workers are: (C(3,2)C(7,1) + C(3,3)) / C(10,3) = 22/120 = 18.3%. Robust aggregation
  assumes lies are independent; deterministic lies are not.
- **Sleepers get a few lies through, and can frame honest workers.** Past probation a
  sleeper is caught after about (1-r)/r unaudited lies; 0-36 lies per run got through. Two
  colluding sleepers that audit the same honest pair outvote it 2 to 1. Honest workers
  banned: 2 on MNIST, 1 on HalfCheetah, 0 on the GPT. M10 adds a referee that settles
  disputes instead of a vote.

## Do honest workers agree?

Spot checks compare two workers' scores of a pair (rtol 1e-3). That only works if honest
scores do not move with things a worker may change without lying. `python -m
bench.bench_agreement` scores step 0's pairs under each condition:

| workload | condition | max relative difference | median | pairs a 1e-3 audit would flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mnist | repeat | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |

| mnist | threads 1 vs default | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |

| halfcheetah | threads 1 vs default | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |
| tinylm | repeat | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |
| tinylm | one pair per batch | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |
| tinylm | threads 1 vs default | 0.0e+00 | 0.0e+00 | 0% |
| tinylm | bfloat16 vs default | 1.0e-04 | 3.5e-05 | 0% |

On CPU, scores are bit-identical whatever else is in the batch and however many threads run.
bfloat16 against float32 moves the language model's scores by at most 1e-4 relative, ten
times inside the tolerance. Text generation is different: where two logits nearly tie,
another precision or device can pick another greedy token, and the text and its score follow
(M10 measures 3.7% of tokens differing between bfloat16 and float32; on one CPU, batch shape
changed none of 54 generations). M10 handles that with evidence (TOPLOC-style checks of the
generated tokens).

## Production hardening (M10)

### A referee: one honest worker is enough

With `--referee self`, any disagreement between workers is settled by the coordinator scoring
the pair itself (after Verde's refereed delegation) instead of a vote. Same benchmark, the
rows where M8/M9 measured framing (`python -m bench.bench_scale --defenses referee`):

| workload | attack (30%) | defense | metric | lies committed | liars banned | honest banned | work |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST (accuracy) | flip | spot | 0.8779 | 0 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.10x |
| MNIST (accuracy) | flip | referee | 0.8779 | 0 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.10x |
| MNIST (accuracy) | sleeper | spot | 0.878 | 6 | 9/9 | 2 | 1.11x |
| MNIST (accuracy) | sleeper | referee | 0.8775 | 7 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.11x |
| HalfCheetah (return) | flip | spot | 3693 | 0 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.11x |
| HalfCheetah (return) | flip | referee | 3693 | 0 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.11x |
| HalfCheetah (return) | sleeper | spot | 3548 | 25 | 9/9 | 1 | 1.11x |
| HalfCheetah (return) | sleeper | referee | 3629 | 25 | 9/9 | 0 | 1.11x |
| char GPT (val loss) | flip | spot | 3.162 | 0 | 6/6 | 0 | 1.11x |
| char GPT (val loss) | flip | referee | 3.162 | 0 | 6/6 | 0 | 1.11x |
| char GPT (val loss) | sleeper | spot | 3.169 | 28 | 6/6 | 0 | 1.11x |
| char GPT (val loss) | sleeper | referee | 3.205 | 29 | 6/6 | 0 | 1.11x |

Colluding sleepers can no longer outvote an honest worker: no honest worker was banned, and
every liar still was, at the same 1.1x work. The referee does not change how many lies a
sleeper slips in before it is caught (that is the audit rate's job: 28 vs 29 on the GPT).
It does change which pairs they land on, because disputes no longer send a pair to a third
worker; on the GPT the two sleeper rows end at 3.169 and 3.205, and with 2 seeds we cannot
tell whether that gap is more than run-to-run noise.

### Checking generated text instead of generating it again

For an LLM, two honest workers do not always produce the same text: where two logits nearly
tie, another precision or GPU can pick another greedy token (the table below: 3.7% of tokens
differ between bfloat16 and float32), and plain score comparison would ban honest workers.
Workers therefore send the token ids they generated, and an auditor
checks them with one teacher-forced pass (each token within 0.5 of the top logit) and grades
that text (TOPLOC, INTELLECT-2, does the same for untrusted inference). Each opinion is judged
by its own evidence, so two honest texts both stand. `python -m bench.bench_verify`, the
architecture of Qwen2.5-0.5B (random weights), 8 rows, 64-token prompts, 64 new tokens:

| | value |
|---|---|
| generate 8 rows x 64 tokens (KV cache) | 12.09 s |
| check them: one teacher-forced pass | 3.0 s (0.25x) |
| honest token's gap to the top logit, same precision | max 0 |
| same, checked in bfloat16 against float32 | max 0.031, nonzero at 3.7% of tokens |

On a CPU, a forward pass over prompt and completion costs a sizable fraction of generating
the completion, because both are compute-bound; on a GPU, generation is bound by memory
traffic per token and the check should be far cheaper (to be measured with `bench/gpu`).
Honest tokens sit at the top logit (gap 0) when the auditor runs the same precision, and
within 0.03 of it when it runs bfloat16 against float32: the 0.5 tolerance leaves a wide
margin, while a forger can only swap a token for a near-tie.

### The same base model everywhere

The run's state holds only the trained Delta, so two workers with different base weights
share every digest and still generate different text. Our own threaded tests hit this: the
test workers built their models at the same time from one global random generator, got
different weights, and banned each other. Workers now report a fingerprint of their base model
(the hub revision, or a hash of the weights; a revision does not depend on the dtype), the
first one sets the run's, and a worker with another is refused with a clear message.

### End to end

A coordinator with join tokens, TLS on both transports, spot checks with a referee and
metrics; three honest workers and one liar, all with tokens, over `tcps://`: the liar was
banned in step 0 ("contradicted by the referee") and its token revoked; the honest workers
finished with the coordinator's exact digest, `seedling replay` rebuilt it from the journal,
and `/metrics` (over HTTPS) showed the ban, the revoked token and 1.35 scores per committed
pair (probation dominates a 30-step run). The same flows are tests in
`tests/test_m10_production.py`.
